;; The formats on the Format Deck page (/formats). Edit this file and the page restyles itself.
;; Lines starting with ;; are notes and never show up on the page.
;;
;; Each format is a block that starts with ## and the format's name. The lines under it are:
;;   category: Contrast        the group it sits in. Spell it the same way in every format of that group.
;;   how: One or two sentences on how the format works.
;;   template: A line of the post. Put {a label} where the writer types something in.
;;                             One "template:" line per line of the post. An empty "template:" is a blank line.
;;   tip: One or two sentences of advice. (Optional.)
;;
;; The same {label} used twice is one blank that fills in twice (handy for callbacks).
;; The post must fit in 300 characters with about 40 characters in each blank. The test suite checks this.
;; In how and tip, a link looks like [text](/tracer) or [text](https://example.com).
;; Categories show up on the page in the order they first appear here.
;;
;; Rules for what goes in (same as the Mashup Machine's topics): no slurs, no named real people,
;; nothing about real tragedies, nothing explicit. Do not use em dashes or en dashes.

## Nobody / Me
category: Contrast
how: Describe a quiet, ordinary world, then show your own very specific, unrequested behavior.
template: Nobody:
template: Me: {an oddly specific thing you do}
tip: If the detail would fit anyone, cut it. "Overthinking" is a mood. "Rehearsing a rebuttal for a stranger" is a person.

## Expectation / Reality
category: Contrast
how: Pair a grand plan with what actually happened. The gap is the joke, so keep the plan sincere and the outcome small and exact.
template: Expectation: {the grand plan}
template: Reality: {the small, exact outcome}
tip: Give both lines the same shape so the second one visibly falls over.

## Before / After
category: Contrast
how: Two lines around one small event that changed how you see ordinary things. The event appears in both lines, so you only type it once.
template: Before {the event}: {how things used to be}
template: After {the event}: {how things are now}
tip: Keep the event tiny. A small trigger with a huge change is the whole engine.

## Translation Guide
category: Contrast
how: Take something polite people say and translate it into what they actually mean. It works best when the real meaning is petty and not mean.
template: "{a polite phrase}" means "{what it really means}"
tip: Choose a phrase people really say, so the reader hears it in someone's voice.

## Two Types of People
category: Contrast
how: Split the world into two groups, where the second group is the point. Make the first side reasonable and the second delightfully specific.
template: There are two types of people in the world: {type one} and {type two}.
tip: Make the second type longer than the first. The extra words are the punchline.

## Ranking
category: Lists
how: A short ranked list whose last item breaks the pattern. The last entry should be longer, odder, or more personal than the rest.
template: Ranking {the things}, worst to best:
template: 1. {worst}
template: 2. {bad}
template: 3. {good}
template: 4. {best}
tip: Write the best item first, then build the other three around it.

## Tier List
category: Lists
how: Sort things into tiers where the top and bottom entries tell the joke. Three tiers are enough.
template: {the things} tier list
template: S: {the top pick}
template: A: {a decent pick}
template: F: {the worst pick}
tip: Give S and A boring entries and let F carry it. Or flip it and make the S tier the strange one.

## Bingo Card
category: Lists
how: List predictable moments from one situation, then end on a free space that says the quiet part.
template: {a situation} bingo:
template: {square one} | {square two} | {square three}
template: FREE SPACE: {the free space}
tip: Squares should be things everyone in that situation has seen. The free space can be sad or silly.

## Award Ceremony
category: Lists
how: Give an award to an unlikely winner and explain it with a reason that is too exact. The reason is the joke.
template: And the award for {the award} goes to {the winner}, for {the reason}.
tip: Word the award as grandly as you can, then let the reason be small.

## Pros and Cons
category: Lists
how: A decision list where the con quietly cancels the pro, often by restating it. One line each, said calmly.
template: Thinking about {a big decision}.
template: Pros: {a lovely pro}
template: Cons: {a calm, devastating con}
tip: Say the con in the same voice as the pro. Do not signal the joke.

## Fake Headline
category: Fake documents
how: Write a news headline where a small, ordinary event is treated as big news. Use headline grammar: present tense, short words, a semicolon or colon.
template: LOCAL {person} {does something ordinary}; {official reaction}
tip: Put the ordinary part before the semicolon and the absurd consequence after it. Capital letters help.

## One-Star Review
category: Fake documents
how: Review something that cannot be reviewed, or review a normal thing for the wrong reason. Stay sincere and a little aggrieved.
template: ★☆☆☆☆ {the thing being reviewed}
template: "{a review title}"
template: {the complaint}
tip: Include one fair compliment near the end. The generosity makes the rest funnier.

## Official Notice
category: Fake documents
how: A posted sign with a mild rule that hints at a bigger history. The reader fills in the backstory.
template: NOTICE: {the rule}.
template: {the hint of a larger history}
tip: Keep a flat, official tone. The more history you imply, the less you should explain it.

## Error Message
category: Fake documents
how: A fake system message that describes a human problem. The code lends credibility and the plain line underneath carries the joke.
template: Error {a code}: {what the system says}
template: {what it means for you}
tip: Use real-sounding codes (404, 418, 500) and keep the system's wording stiff.

## Patch Notes
category: Fake documents
how: Describe your life, a person, or an object as software being updated. Start with technical-sounding changes and end with personal ones.
template: Patch notes v{a version number}:
template: - {a believable change}
template: - {a petty change}
template: - Fixed a bug where {an absurd bug}
tip: End on the bug. It should be the thing everyone secretly wishes they could fix.

## Customer Service
category: Dialogue
how: A short exchange where a representative answers a normal complaint with total calm and zero help.
template: Me: {my complaint}
template: Them: {the calm, unhelpful reply}
tip: Make the reply sound like a policy. The agent never apologizes.

## Overheard
category: Dialogue
how: Report one line of dialogue with no context. The missing context does the work.
template: Overheard at {a place}:
template: "{a line out of context}"
tip: The line should sound like part of a story you would pay to hear. Do not explain it.

## Interview Question
category: Dialogue
how: A job interview where your answer is honest, strange, or both. A fixed question with a fresh answer is an old trick for a reason.
template: Interviewer: {the question}
template: Me: {my answer}
tip: Answer quickly and flatly. Hesitation reads as a bit. Confidence reads as a person.

## Brain Dialogue
category: Dialogue
how: You argue with your own brain. The brain brings up something old or odd at the worst time, and your reply never stops it.
template: Brain: {an unhelpful thought}
template: Me: {a reasonable reply}
template: Brain: {the escalation}
tip: Three lines at most, and the brain always gets the last word.

## Talking Object
category: Dialogue
how: Say something to an object and let it answer back. Give the object a human flaw such as pride, laziness, or passive aggression.
template: Me: {what I say to the object}
template: {the object}: {what it says back}
tip: Let the object stay calm. An object that explains itself is funnier than one that rants.

## Which One of You
category: Questions
how: Call out a small, unnamed crime to a group. Everyone feels vaguely guilty.
template: Which one of you {did the small crime}? {a worrying detail}
tip: The smaller the crime, the bigger the energy of the accusation.

## Be Honest
category: Questions
how: Ask the whole timeline an honest question about a shared small shame. The answers in the replies are the second half of the joke.
template: Be honest: {an everyday question}
tip: Pick a habit people are quietly proud of or quietly embarrassed by, not a rare event.

## Rate It
category: Questions
how: Offer something for rating, then give details that make a fair rating impossible. The replies do the rest.
template: Rate my {the thing} out of 10:
template: {the details}
tip: End on a detail that makes people want to answer with both a number and an opinion.

## Fill in the Blank
category: Questions
how: Give people the start of a sentence and let them finish it. Leave room for answers better than any you would write.
template: {the start of a sentence} ___.
template: {where to answer}
tip: The best prompts are specific enough to be funny and open enough to reward the weird answer.

## Quote Post Game Starter
category: Questions
how: Open a quote post game with a topic, one clear rule, and your own first entry so people know the tone.
template: QUOTE POST GAME
template: Topic: {the topic}
template: Rule: {one clear rule}
template: First entry: {your first entry}
tip: Keep the rule to one sentence and check it for loopholes. Afterward, the [Quote Post Game Tracer](/tracer) shows every quote as a tree.

## Pun With a Question
category: Wordplay
how: The classic question-and-answer pun. A short question and an instant answer.
template: {a question}
template: {the pun answer}
tip: Put the pun word last. A pun followed by even one more word dies.

## Dictionary Entry
category: Wordplay
how: A new word, a fake definition, and a sentence that uses it. It works best when the word sounds like it already exists.
template: {the word} (n.): {the definition}
template: "{a sentence using it}"
tip: Make the example sentence mundane. Treat the word as if it has always been real.

## Portmanteau
category: Wordplay
how: Blend two words into a new one and define it. If people say "oh no" at the blend, you are on the right track.
template: {word one} + {word two} = {the blend}
template: (n.) {what it means}
tip: Say the blend out loud. If you stumble on it, so will the reader.

## Rename It
category: Wordplay
how: Give something ordinary a new, more honest name. The reason is optional, but it is often where the joke lives.
template: Renaming {the thing} to "{the new name}" because {the reason}.
tip: Make the new name long and grand. Short names rarely read as jokes.

## If It Were
category: Wordplay
how: Compare two unlike things by naming the specific version of the second. The specific answer is the whole joke.
template: If {the thing} were a {a kind of thing}, it would be {the specific version}.
tip: "A sad one" is a shrug. "The one with a dead plant in the lobby" is a picture.

## Rule of Three
category: Escalation
how: Two items set a pattern and the third breaks it. The pattern is the setup and the break is the punchline.
template: {first item}, {second item}, and {third item}.
tip: Make the first two similar in weight and length. Make the third shorter or odder.

## Anti-Climax
category: Escalation
how: Build up something grand, then land on something tiny. The bigger the build, the smaller the letdown can be.
template: {a grand build-up}, only to {a tiny letdown}.
tip: Spend most of your words on the build-up and very few on the letdown.

## Running Bit
category: Escalation
how: Say the same calm sentence in a worsening situation. The phrase stays fixed while the world falls apart around it. You type the phrase once and it fills in twice.
template: Day 1: {a small problem}. I said "{the repeated phrase}."
template: Day 9: {a much worse problem}. I said "{the repeated phrase}."
tip: The phrase should never change. The situation does all the escalating.

## Hill to Die On
category: Escalation
how: List the things you will defend to the end: two serious principles, then one tiny petty one.
template: Hills I will die on:
template: - {a serious principle}
template: - {another serious principle}
template: - {a tiny petty thing}
tip: Make the petty hill the most detailed one. Specificity is the joke.

## How To
category: Escalation
how: Give instructions that start normal and end up somewhere else. Each step should be a little odder than the last.
template: How to {reach a goal}:
template: 1. {a normal step}
template: 2. {a slightly odd step}
template: 3. {a step that makes no sense}
tip: Keep step 3 in the same calm voice as step 1.

## Nature Documentary
category: Tiny scenes
how: Narrate a mundane human like a wildlife film. The calmer the narrator, the funnier the tiny stakes.
template: Here we see the {a creature} in its natural habitat, {doing something mundane}.
template: {a dramatic narration line}
tip: Speak as if it is life or death and the audience is holding its breath.

## Dear Diary
category: Tiny scenes
how: A diary entry about a long, strange situation with a very small observation at the end.
template: Dear diary, day {a number} of {a situation}.
template: {the observation}
tip: End on an observation that is tiny and uncomfortable, not a big reveal.

## Future Archaeologists
category: Tiny scenes
how: Imagine people in the far future misreading an ordinary object. The wrong conclusion has to be reasonable, which is what makes it funny.
template: Future archaeologists will find {an ordinary object} and conclude {the wrong conclusion}.
tip: The wrong conclusion should make sense from the outside while being totally wrong.

## Confession
category: Tiny scenes
how: Admit something small and specific with the weight of a major confession.
template: I have a confession:
template: {the confession}
tip: Confess a habit, not a crime. The smaller the secret, the better.

## Tiny Scene
category: Tiny scenes
how: Describe a person doing something small, then reveal one absurd fact they do not know. The reader enjoys knowing more than the character.
template: A {a kind of person} is {doing something small}.
template: They do not know that {the absurd truth}.
tip: Narrate in the flat voice of a news report.
