How Rush Hour works

The idea

Type a Bluesky handle and Rush Hour reads that account's recent posts. It draws a grid of the hours of the week, showing when the posts go up and when they get the most likes. A second panel, "What Lands," compares the top 10% of posts against the rest.

The three steps

  1. Type a handle. With or without the @, or paste a link to the profile. It works on any public account, not only your own.
  2. Wait a moment. It reads the newest 500 posts first. Press "Load 500 more" to go further back, up to 5,000.
  3. Look around. Switch the grid between how many posts and the average likes, and between your own time and UTC.

What it counts

  • The grid counts the account's own posts and quote posts. Replies and reposts are left out, so a night of chatting doesn't hide when the real posts go up.
  • "Does best" means likes. Reposts, quotes and replies are not added in.
  • Average likes for a square needs at least 3 posts in it to count toward the "best time" line. Squares with fewer are drawn with a dotted edge. A few posts in a square can be luck, not a pattern.
  • What Lands looks at replies too, so it can tell you if replies do better or worse. It needs at least 20 posts. The top 10% is the 10% with the most likes, and the rest is everything else.
  • A picture means an image, a video or a GIF. A post's opening word is the first word of its text.

Good to know

  • Public posts only, read straight from Bluesky by your browser. Nothing is sent to our server or stored.
  • Like counts are as of the moment the posts were read. New posts haven't had time to collect likes yet, so very recent posts look weaker than they will.
  • The newest posts come first. If an account has more posts than were read, the picture is of its recent past, not its whole life.
  • Hours are in your own time zone unless you switch to UTC.

What this asks for

  • Sign-in: none. No password, no permissions.
  • Reads: one public profile and the account's public posts, the same as someone who isn't logged in.
  • Stored: nothing. Your browser reads the posts and does the counting here.

Only dates, like counts, post length, and each post's first word are used. No post text is shown or kept.
